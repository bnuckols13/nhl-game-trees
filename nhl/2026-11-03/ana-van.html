Game tree · version 2 · built from the plays, then simulated · crease correction applied

Anaheim's boom runs through one power play unit, and this tree's biggest branch was an artifact

Puck drop 9:00 PM ET, the slate's last game, so late swap stays live on these slots after the 7:00 PM main lock. Total 6.5. Implied goals: Anaheim 3.47, Vancouver 3.03, priced by the market with Lukas Dostal and Leevi Merilainen in mind. Neither starts: the 4:00 PM sweep put Laurent Brossoit in Anaheim's net and Kevin Lankinen in Vancouver's, and the desk's correction memo re-ran this tree with the real creases. This page carries both: the tree as built, and what the correction does to it. Method: every goal in two seasons of play-by-play for both teams (189 games), capture measured on shared goals, then 25,000 simulated games, backtested before any number was trusted.

Level 2 · Capture, from the plays — who actually pays together

The correlation lives on the power play. Vancouver's power play goals pay three participants 93.4% of the time, and its first power play unit takes 63.1% of the points on the goals it exists for; Anaheim's unit takes 65.0% of its power play points. Even-strength lines almost never pay two members on the same goal: Vancouver's first line did it on 12 of 178 even-strength goals, Anaheim's listed second line on 20 of 227. An even-strength line stack is a bet on scorer concentration across separate goals. A power play stack is buying a correlation that fires nearly every time the unit scores.

The assist networks are stars, not webs. Anaheim's runs through Gauthier as scorer and LaCombe as assister: Gauthier shares 25 goals with Carlsson and 23 with Sennecke, LaCombe shares 22 with Gauthier, and LaCombe fed Gauthier 16 times. Vancouver's runs through Pettersson as assister: he shares 22 goals with DeBrusk and 22 with Hronek, and fed DeBrusk 15 times. Pair capture beyond those hubs is thin, which is why the leaves below are built on units, not pairs.

Brossoit's shape is the as-built Anaheim shape shifted down by roughly six points in Anaheim win scripts and five to seven in Vancouver win scripts, with the mass moved negative: at his measured rate he is negative in every Vancouver win script, and Vancouver win scripts are 57% of the raw corrected world. The desk's verdict: no Anaheim goalie exposure survives. At his measured rate Brossoit is the worst priced crease in this game, a median night of zero at $7,600. Even at the shrunk end of his band he is only Dostal-minus, and worse exactly where the close-win leaf lives. There is no point in the band where rostering him is a positive-expectation play; the best case is harmless filler inside a rout construction. Every Anaheim stack lineup should be built goalie-free from this game and paired with a confirmed crease from another game: in the measured history, winners pair their goalie with a different game 71% of the time and against their own stack zero times.

Every point source, audited — verified against the FanDuel rules; nothing else scores

Skaters: goal 12, assist 8, shot on goal 1.6, blocked shot 1.6, shorthanded point +2, power play point +0.5. Goalies: win 12, save 0.8, goal against minus 4, shutout 8. That is the whole table. Hits, takeaways, giveaways, plus-minus, faceoffs, penalty minutes, hat tricks, overtime losses and shootout goals or saves all pay nothing. One open item, flagged unverified: whether FanDuel credits a goalie assist is unsettled; the simulation does not score them.

In 2,411 player-games across both rosters, no skater reached 20 FanDuel points without scoring a point. The ceiling without one is 14.4, a five-shot, four-block game, and that is also the largest no-point game in the record. Inside defensemen's 20+ games, points supplied 70.1% of the score, shots 16.2% and blocks 13.7%. Shots and blocks raise the floor and fatten the middle; they do not create a 20+ path on their own. The power play bonus itself is decoration, worth at most 0.19 points a game to its biggest earner, Carlsson: the first unit's value is goal and assist volume, not the bonus. Blocks follow the score state: leading teams block more, Anaheim at .588 per opponent shot while leading against .549 while trailing, Vancouver .552 against .461, which is why cheap defensemen on the leading team earn quietly in the grind scripts.

Size this game's exposure as if both ends of Brossoit's band are live, because they are.

How far to trust it: the tree's backtest, 64 team games simulated walk-forward from pre-game information only, says the engine's shape is nearly Poisson at equal inputs, so the machinery adds structure, not manufactured tails. Its per-game forecasts did not beat the base rate on the headline probability (Brier .215, level with climatology), so read any single script price with about 3 points of tolerance and trust rankings over point values. Unit and player medians run hot and are haircut 15 to 20% in the desk's use. The correction's provenance is tighter: its engine replicates the as-built run exactly, maximum script difference 0.000, so every delta on this page is the goalie swap and nothing else; and its script moves run 5 to 20 points, far outside the tolerance, so the material calls do not depend on it. The one number that cannot be tightened tonight is Brossoit himself: two starts, 47 shots, and if anything the raw run understates the chaos, because the engine's score-state feedback caps how far a bad crease can run a total up.
