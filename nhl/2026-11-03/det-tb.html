Level 2 · Capture, from the plays

Tampa's network is a star around Kucherov: 45 shared goals with Hagel, 41 with Guentzel, 35 with Point. Detroit's runs through Raymond. The doctrine from the worked example, that shared-goal correlation lives on the power play, holds here with one measured exception. Tampa's first line is the strongest even-strength line on either built tree, taking 30.4% of the points on even-strength goals and paying two members on 43 of 245 goals, against Detroit's first line at 20.8% and 11 of 191. Kucherov's line genuinely pays two members at even strength at a rate no other even-strength line in the build matches. Detroit's power play unit is the diffuse one: it takes 55.6% of the points on its power play goals, the lowest share in the build, and pays zero members on 8 of its 65 power play goals. A Detroit power play stack is a bet on volume, the game's highest expected power play goals, more than on concentration.

Detroit shoots 29.6 a game, so his save volume survives even Tampa's mid scripts. He is likely, not confirmed, as of the tree's run; the Hildeby branch is genuinely near-neutral, so if he sits, Tampa's skater leaves keep their prices and the goalie slot gets worse by a tier. Gibson is expected for Detroit (today's sources correct the morning file: Tarasov started Monday and won it, which strengthens the freshness case for Gibson), and he is rosterable only inside Detroit-win leaves, never naked: his median night is 5.6. The Tarasov branch carries the tree's softest number, stated plainly in the file: on pooled two-season save percentage the crease swap moves Tampa's mean by about 0.08 goals, nothing; on 2026-27 form alone it would move it by over a goal and upgrade every Detroit leaf. Which Gibson is real this season is the question, and the T-60 starter flag decides which branch the book prices.
