NHL · standing reference · research memo 26

Winning an 11-game NHL Main takes about 242 points, and the winner is built the same way at every slate size

Measured, not estimated: 292 FanDuel GPP fields pulled lineup by lineup across 227 slates, from the 381 completed Main slates STAT-API carries for 2024-25 through 2026-27 to date. Its contest archive covers 242 of those slates, and the three fields the desk first measured by hand reproduce to the tenth inside this pipeline, so the method checks against itself.

There is a direct twin for a big Tuesday slate: the October 27, 2026 $20K Breakaway ran on 11 games with a 5,362-entry field, and it took 239.3 to win and 193.0 for the top 1%.

Ninety-six percent of winners hold at least four players at 20 or more. And in 280 winners whose lineups could be classified, the biggest stack was the team playing against the winner's own goalie zero times: 29% of the time the stack was the goalie's own team, and the rest of the time it came from a different game entirely. Nobody wins these by stacking skaters into their own goalie.

What field size explains, and what it does not

Field size is real but modest: the correlation between log field size and the winning score is 0.34, worth about 18.2 points per tenfold increase in entries, and slate size adds about 3.3 points per game. Together they explain under a quarter of the variance (R² 0.234). Most of what moves a winning score is the night's scoring environment, which no pre-slate number fully sees. That is why each slate page prices its games as distributions and scripts, not as point forecasts.

Caveats, printed with the numbers. The archive is sampled: 139 of the 381 slates carry no contests in the API, so this is the population of retrievable fields, not of all fields ever run. Playoff slates sit inside the season windows. The lineups endpoint caps at 10,000 rows per contest, which affects 18 contests; winners, cuts and min-cash stay exact there, and medians are read by rank position.
